# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 – Line Graph Template

**Target:** Band 7–8 | **Length:** 160–170 words (minimum 150) | **Time:** 20 minutes

Use four paragraphs. **No separate conclusion.** The figures below are illustrative practice data.

## 1. Introduction (20–30 words)

**Purpose:** Paraphrase the question.

**Fixed template:**

The line graph provides information about + **[topic]** + **[place/categories]** + **[time period]**.

**Example:**

> The line graph provides information about the percentage of people using the internet in three different countries between 2000 and 2020.

**Tense:** Present simple to introduce the graph (*provides*), even if the data is historical.

## 2. Overall (30–40 words)

**Purpose:** Summarise the main trends, without detailed numbers.

**Fixed template:**

Overall, it is clear that + **[main trend]** + while/whereas + **[second major trend]**.

**Example:**

> Overall, it is clear that internet usage increased in all three countries throughout the period. Country A experienced the most significant growth and finished with the highest percentage, whereas Country C remained the lowest.

**Tense:** Past simple for data from 2000–2020 (*increased, experienced, finished, remained*).

**Tip:** Avoid exact percentages in the overview; focus on major patterns.

## 3. Body Paragraph 1 (45–55 words)

**Purpose:** Describe the first group of important trends using figures.

**Fixed templates:**

- **Looking at the details,** + [category] + [starting value].
- **The figure increased from** + [starting value] + **to** + [ending value].
- **This was followed by** + [next category or trend, if the ranking fits].
- **In comparison,** + [another category and comparison].

**Example:**

> Looking at the details, internet usage in Country A stood at 20% in 2000 before rising steadily to 55% in 2010. It then increased further to 90% by 2020. In comparison, Country B started at a higher level of 40% but grew more slowly, reaching 75% at the end of the period.

**Tense:** Past simple (*stood, increased, started, grew*).

## 4. Body Paragraph 2 (45–55 words)

**Purpose:** Describe the remaining trends and meaningful comparisons.

**Fixed templates:**

- **By contrast,** + [remaining category and starting value].
- **Meanwhile,** + [another trend or change].
- **Similarly,** + [similar trend, if applicable].
- **Whereas** + [category A and trend], + [category B and contrasting trend].

**Example:**

> By contrast, Country C had the lowest internet usage in 2000, at just 10%. However, its figure rose consistently, reaching 35% in 2010 and 65% in 2020. Meanwhile, Country A overtook Country B between 2005 and 2010, eventually finishing 15 percentage points higher.

**Tense:** Past simple (*had, rose, overtook*).

---

## Vocabulary for line graphs

| Trend | Useful words |
|---|---|
| Increase | increased, rose, grew, climbed |
| Decrease | decreased, fell, declined, dropped |
| Stable | remained stable, stayed constant |
| Fluctuation | fluctuated, varied |
| Highest point | peaked at, reached a peak of |
| Lowest point | reached a low of, bottomed out at |

## Tense reference

| Data period | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Past | Past simple | Internet usage **rose** steadily. |
| Present / ongoing | Present simple | Internet usage **is** higher in Country A. |
| Future forecast | Prediction | Internet usage **is expected to rise**. |
| Past and future | Mixed | Usage **increased** and **is projected to grow** further. |

**2. Overall**

**3. Body Paragraph 1**  
Looking at the details, + **[category and starting value]**. The figure increased from + **[starting value]** + to + **[ending value]**. In comparison, + **[another category and comparison]**.

**4. Body Paragraph 2**  
By contrast, + **[remaining category and value]**. Meanwhile, + **[another trend]**. Whereas + **[final comparison]**.

**Remember:** These are flexible sentence patterns, not sentences to copy mechanically. Use language that accurately describes the graph. In line graphs, prioritise **changes over time**, including starting and ending values, peaks, declines, and fluctuations.
